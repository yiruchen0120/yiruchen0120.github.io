## About Me

I am a PhD researcher in Economics at Leeds University Business School, University of Leeds, United Kingdom.

My research interests lie in **monetary economics and international finance**, with a particular focus on **central bank communication, monetary policy transmission, and global financial markets**.

My research examines how central banks communicate monetary policy information, how financial markets interpret different dimensions of central bank communication, and how the resulting effects are transmitted across countries and financial markets.

Methodologically, I am interested in combining empirical approaches in monetary economics with **natural language processing (NLP), large language models (LLMs), machine learning, and high-frequency financial data**.

My PhD research is supervised by Professor Muhammad Ali Nasir and Dr Xingjie Wei.

## Working Papers

### When Words Are Not Enough: Emotional Spillovers from Fed Chair Communication to Global Financial Markets

**Yiru Chen**, Muhammad Ali Nasir, and Xingjie Wei

This paper investigates how the textual, vocal, and facial emotional signals conveyed by Federal Reserve Chairs during FOMC press conferences influence international financial markets.

Using multimodal emotion measures and high-frequency financial market data, we examine the international transmission of central bank communication beyond conventional monetary policy announcements.

Our analysis explores cross-country heterogeneity, global risk conditions, and the interaction between different communication modalities.

*Working paper.*

## Research in Progress

### Central Bank Mandates and Communication Strategies

This project investigates how the content, scope, and hierarchy of statutory central bank mandates shape communication strategies and reputational priorities.

It examines how central banks respond when macroeconomic outcomes deviate from their policy objectives, with particular attention to the Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank, and the People's Bank of China.

### International Transmission of Central Bank Communication

This project explores how multimodal signals from Federal Reserve Chair communication propagate across international financial markets, and how cross-border financial linkages and global risk conditions shape these spillovers.
