My research interests lie in monetary economics and international finance, with a particular focus on central bank communication, monetary policy transmission, and international financial markets.

Methodologically, I combine empirical approaches in monetary economics with natural language processing (NLP), machine learning, multimodal analysis, and high-frequency financial data.

## Working Papers

### When Words Are Not Enough: Emotional Spillovers from Fed Chair Communication to Global Financial Markets

**Yiru Chen**, Muhammad Ali Nasir, and Xingjie Wei

This paper examines how textual, vocal, and facial emotional signals conveyed by Federal Reserve Chairs during FOMC press conferences influence international financial markets.

Using multimodal emotion measures and high-frequency financial market data, we investigate the international spillovers of central bank communication, cross-modal interactions, and the role of global financial conditions and country-specific vulnerabilities.
